Practice-based Research

Practice-based Research in Japanese Subculture and ACG Culture

My research on Japanese subculture and ACG (Animation, Comics, Games) culture combines textual and theoretical analysis with sustained creative practice. I conceptualize cultural production not as a supplementary interest, but as a methodological site where cultural meanings, identities, and affective relations are produced, negotiated, and embodied.

Research framing

What this approach examines

  • How Japanese ACG culture is lived, reproduced, and recontextualized across transnational and digital environments.
  • How embodied, performative, and platform-mediated practices shape contemporary youth, Generation Z, and fandom cultures.
  • How identities and affective relations emerge through participatory cultural practices.
Platform-mediated practice

Platform-based Performative Practice: V-Tuber

Focus: NicoNico · Bilibili · virtual performance · identity construction

V-Tuber culture—originally conceptualized as Vチューバー（V-Tuber）—emerged from native content practices on the Japanese platform NicoNico and constitutes an important subcultural branch within Japanese ACG culture. My practice-based research engages with this cultural form through sustained V-Tuber activity on the Chinese video-sharing platform Bilibili, situating Japanese-originated virtual performance within a transnational platform environment.

What I do

  • Participate across the full production process of a V-Tuber persona (concept development → visual design → Live2D model production → software application).
  • Maintain sustained V-Tuber activity on Bilibili to examine platform-mediated participation in practice.

What I examine

  • How virtual identities are constructed, mediated, and sustained within contemporary ACG-related platform cultures.
  • How Japanese-originated virtual performance is localized and recontextualized within transnational platform environments.

Genre focus: 歌ってみた (utatte-mita)

Within this context, I deliberately adopt the Japanese subcultural performance genre known as 歌ってみた (utatte-mita, “singing covers”), a well-established expressive form within NicoNico and broader Japanese ACG culture. Rather than treating anime song performance as an independent musical activity, 歌ってみた is analyzed here as an affective and participatory mode embedded within V-Tuber culture, enabling a deeper practice-based engagement with Japanese ACG aesthetics, collective memory, and fandom practices.

Embodied practice

Cosplay as Embodied Research Practice

Focus: embodiment · visual semiotics · fan creativity · character-based identification

In contrast to platform-based virtual practice, my research also incorporates cosplay—コスプレ（Cosplay）—as an embodied and materially grounded research practice. Rather than focusing on public performance or event participation, cosplay is approached here as a mode of embodied interpretation through which fictional ACG characters are translated into bodily presence, visual form, and affective stance.

What I do

  • Conduct costume-making, character visualization, and embodied experimentation as practice-based inquiry.
  • Treat visual outputs (photos and documentation) as research materials for analytical reflection.

What I examine

  • How character design and visual semiotics are translated into embodied presence.
  • How bodies, media texts, and affective stances interact within ACG-related cosplay cultures.

Interpretive note

Within Japanese “two-dimensional” (subcultural) and cosplay cultures, real-world gender is often de-emphasized or rendered secondary to character-based identification. As a result, cosplay practices are generally not constrained by the cosplayer’s biological or social gender. Grounded in a shared affection for Japanese ACG culture and a strong sense of fandom-based identification, cosplay participants frequently suspend their “three-dimensional” (real-world) identity markers and engage with one another primarily within the “two-dimensional” cultural imaginary. Through this process, cosplay contributes to the formation of distinctive subcultural social networks (circle layers), in which mutual recognition is structured around characters, aesthetics, and shared cultural knowledge rather than real-world demographic attributes.

Synthesis

Synthesis

Platform-based V-Tuber practice and embodied cosplay practice together constitute a multi-modal framework for examining contemporary Japanese ACG culture as a lived and practiced cultural system.

Key takeaway

  • These practices foreground how cultural identities and group affiliations are formed through embodied, affective, and mediated participation.
  • They highlight the role of Japanese ACG culture in shaping subcultural belonging, creative labor, and identity formation within transnational youth and fandom cultures.

Situated within the broader context of Generation Z, subcultural fandoms, and participatory fan communities, this research foregrounds how cultural identities and group affiliations are formed through embodied, affective, and mediated practices.

By examining both virtual and physical modes of participation, this approach highlights the ways in which Japanese ACG culture operates as a lived and practiced cultural system, shaping subcultural belonging, creative labor, and identity formation within transnational youth and fandom cultures.
